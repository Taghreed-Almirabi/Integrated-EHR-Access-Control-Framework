Overview

Master of Science in Information Technology capstone, University of the People (MSIT 5910, 2026), supervised by Dr. Stella T. Whyte.

Problem

Government hospitals in Saudi Arabia are moving to shared electronic records while the Personal Data Protection Law and the National Cybersecurity Authority's Essential Cybersecurity Controls raise the bar for who may see what. Flat role permissions either over-expose records or block clinicians when minutes matter.

Approach

A single policy engine evaluates each request in a fixed order: account and authentication state, emergency status, role permissions, patient context, duty period, clinician–patient assignment, then risk. High-risk contexts step up to MFA instead of a flat deny. Emergency access is limited to clinical roles, lasts 15 minutes and must carry a justification. Every decision is written to SQLite with an HMAC-SHA256 checksum.

Result

A Python 3.12 prototype released in six tagged versions, verified by 25 pytest cases across unit, integration, system and acceptance levels, with 100% decision accuracy on 36 labelled scenarios, 3.2 ms mean and 4.2 ms p99 latency and about 312 audited requests per second on a laptop. Two healthcare IT experts rated it 4.85 / 5, quality was reviewed against ISO/IEC 25010, and the audit checksum was shown to detect tampering.

How a request is decided

The engine stops at the first failing check and records the reason. The order below is the order in the code.

  1. Is the account active?if not: DENY — Account is inactive.
  2. Is the user authenticated?if not: DENY — Authentication is required.
  3. Is a user identifier present?if not: DENY — A valid user identifier is required.
  4. Is this an emergency? Physicians and nurses may break the glass for a clinical action on a named patient with a written justification.if valid: BREAK_GLASS_PERMIT for 15 minutes, flagged for review
  5. Does the role permit the action?if not: DENY — The requested action is not permitted for this role.
  6. Does a patient action name a patient?if not: DENY — A patient identifier is required for this action.
  7. Is the user within an active duty period?if not: DENY — Access is outside the user's active duty period.
  8. Is the clinician assigned to this patient?if not: DENY — The clinician is not assigned to this patient.
  9. Is the context high-risk (export, account management, off-network or unmanaged device) without MFA?if so: STEP_UP_MFA — otherwisePERMIT — Role and contextual access checks passed.

About

I am Taghreed Almirabi, a Master of Science in Information Technology candidate at the University of the People. This project is my capstone, and it grew out of a practical question: how can a hospital protect patient records under Saudi data-protection rules without slowing down the people who treat patients? The answer I built is small enough to read in an afternoon and tested well enough to trust.

The work covers requirements analysis with hospital stakeholders, policy design against the NIST RBAC model and NIST SP 800-63B-4, implementation in Python, automated testing at four levels, performance evaluation, and documentation to ISO/IEC 25010 quality characteristics.
